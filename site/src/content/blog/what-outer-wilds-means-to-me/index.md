SPOILERS BELOW! Don't read if you're considering playing Outer Wilds. And you should consider it. Or watching someone play it, if you don't like holding a controller and such.

As I wrote in [this earlier post](/blog/in-praise-of-pause), a game called Outer Wilds hit me hard, on several levels. So much so that it pushed me to change careers.

Here's what I'll be writing about:

- Existentialism in Outer Wilds - why it's more than just a fun game (and it is a fun game!)
- Why the Nomai chasing the Eye of the Universe, and a moon that only stays put while you're looking at it, hit close to my own persnal+family history with searching for something greater
- The Coming of Age Pilgrimage every Nomai makes to that moon, and the young Nomai who admits outright she's not even sure the Eye is real. Ouch - hits me in the existential feels.
- My tattoo of Solanum, the last Nomai, stranded on the Quantum Moon. Why she's an icon of hope to me, not proof. And that's how my own faith now operates - hope and trust, rather than certainty and proof.
- Outer Wilds non-linear, player-driven storytelling. It has no missions and no set order, you just follow whatever thread you find. Narrative designer [Kelsey Beachum](http://kelseybeachum.com/projects) has written and spoken about exactly her ingenious approach.
